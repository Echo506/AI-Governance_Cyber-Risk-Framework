# AI Governance & Cybersecurity Risk Framework

## Project Charter

### Organization
NovaConnect Services is a fictional telecommunications and enterprise-support organization that manages connectivity services, network incidents, customer support tickets, and operational communications.

### Business Context
NovaConnect plans to introduce a generative AI assistant to support NOC and technical-support teams. The assistant will summarize support tickets, recommend response drafts, classify incidents, and assist with knowledge retrieval.

The initiative aims to reduce administrative workload and accelerate ticket handling. However, the use of AI may introduce cybersecurity, privacy, operational, legal, and reputational risks that must be governed before deployment.

### Project Objective
Design a practical AI governance and cybersecurity risk-management framework that enables NovaConnect to adopt AI responsibly while protecting customer information, operational data, systems, and business continuity.

### Scope
This project covers the governance and risk-management controls for one AI-enabled support assistant used by internal NOC and technical-support personnel.

The scope includes:
- AI governance roles, accountability, and approval workflows
- AI system inventory and data-flow documentation
- AI impact and risk assessment
- Cybersecurity and privacy controls
- Third-party and supply-chain risk management
- Human oversight and acceptable-use requirements
- Incident response, recovery, monitoring, and continuous improvement
- Regulatory and standards crosswalk

### Out of Scope
This project does not develop, train, or deploy an AI model. It does not replace legal advice, security architecture reviews, or formal certification audits.

### Key Stakeholders
- Executive sponsor
- AI governance owner
- Information Security / SOC team
- NOC and Technical Support Operations
- Privacy and Legal / Compliance
- Data and IT platform owners
- Procurement and third-party risk management
- AI provider or cloud service provider
- Internal end users

### Success Criteria
- Every AI use case is documented, approved, and assigned an accountable owner.
- AI systems, datasets, integrations, and vendors are recorded in an inventory.
- High-risk use cases undergo an AI impact assessment before deployment.
- Security, privacy, human-oversight, and incident-management controls are documented.
- Risks are tracked through a risk register with defined owners and treatment actions.
- Key performance indicators and key risk indicators are monitored after deployment.

### Framework Alignment
This project aligns with:
- NIST AI Risk Management Framework: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover
- ISO/IEC 42001 AI Management System principles
- GDPR privacy principles where personal data is processed
- EU AI Act risk-based obligations where applicable
- Colorado SB24-205 high-risk AI governance concepts where applicable

### Guiding Principle
AI adoption must be secure, accountable, transparent, and continuously monitored. AI will support human decisions and operations; it will not independently make high-impact decisions without appropriate authorization and oversight.
